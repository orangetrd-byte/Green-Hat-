Day 1 Guided Path

Use this when you are new, nervous, or taking over a setup. Do one line at a time.

  1. Fill out Job Notes so the part, operation, machine, material, and status are known.
  2. Open Setup Reference and write down work offset, stock, jaws, stickout, coolant, and shop rules.
  3. Use Touch-Off Step 1 through Step 4. Do not skip the X mode and offset checks.
  4. Check Speeds & Feeds against your shop standard or trainer before cutting.
  5. Print or export the handoff summary if another person may run this setup.

Step 1 — Measure Your Stock

Use calipers to measure the actual diameter of your stock where the tool is touching. This is your touch-off diameter.

lightbulb Check X mode first. Many lathes show X as diameter, but some controls can use diameter/radius modes such as G7/G8. Look near the top of the program, the setup sheet, and the active modal display before entering an offset.
search Where to look: Check the first safety lines of the program, the active G-code/modal screen, and your setup sheet. If you are not sure whether X is diameter or radius, stop and ask before typing numbers into the offset page.
my_location

Step 2 — Enter Your Target

Enter the size you want the part to end up at. For a beginner, think of this as "where the finished cut should be," not a button to move the machine yet.

First: touch off and set/confirm the offset on the control.
Then: use this calculator to compare the measured diameter to the target diameter.
Before cutting: air move, single block, and keep feed override low.

Reference

X Diameter vs Radius

X diameter is the full part size. Radial travel is half that change, because the tool moves from centerline outward or inward.

Z Face And Safe Retract

Plan a clear retract path before rapid moves. Move away from jaws, shoulders, chuck, and grooves before moving fast.

Stickout And Jaws

Stickout is unsupported material hanging out of the jaws. Longer stickout can chatter, bend, or create clearance risk.

Control confirmedYou are at the right machine, the right program or screen is active, and the control mode matches what you expect.
StickoutThe amount of part or tool hanging out unsupported. Long stickout can cause chatter, taper, poor finish, or clearance problems.
Work zeroThe part location the program uses as zero. On a lathe this is often the finished face for Z and the spindle centerline for X.
Work offsetA saved position that tells the control where the part zero is. Example: G54 may point to the finished face of the part.
Tool offsetA saved position that tells the control where the tool tip is. Each tool usually has its own offset number.
Wear offsetA small correction used after measuring a cut part. Example: if the diameter is too big, a small X wear correction may bring the next part into tolerance.
OffsetA saved correction that tells the control where the part or tool really is.
Single blockA control mode that runs one program line at a time so the operator can verify each move.
Feed overrideA knob or setting that slows or speeds programmed feed moves. Keep it low while proving a new setup.
Active modal displayThe control screen area that shows the modes currently active, such as feed mode, spindle mode, work offset, and sometimes diameter/radius behavior.
Safety blockThe first lines of a program that set important modes before cutting. Read this area before trusting any move.
  • Confirm jaws, stickout, stock length, and work offset before touching off.
  • Keep a written note of the exact touch-off surface and tool offset used.
  • Check clearance at both X and Z before the first rapid move.
  • After loading a program, single-block the first run with feed override low.
  • Verify spindle direction (M03/M04) matches your operation before starting.
  • X is diameter based on most CNC lathes — a .010 diameter change is .005 radial.
  • Check the safety block, setup sheet, and active modal display for X diameter/radius mode. Some controls use G7/G8, but verify the machine manual.
  • Z face is usually 0.000. Plunging into the face moves Z negative.
  • After touching off, make a short air move before cutting stock.
  • Touch off on a clean, burr-free surface for accuracy.
  • If you re-touch off mid-job, re-verify your offsets on the control.
  • Rapid to a safe X and safe Z before feed moves.
  • Retract X clear before moving Z when shoulders, jaws, or grooves are close.
  • Single block the first run and keep feed override low until motion is proven.
  • Never rapid over a shoulder — plan your retract path before running.

GripHow much material the jaws are holding. Too little grip can be unsafe.

  • Check threading tool angle and center height — both matter for thread form.
